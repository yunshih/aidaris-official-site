## September 2026 — AI Can Build Anything. It Can't Tell You What to Build.

**ZH title:** AI 什麼都能做，但它不知道你該做什麼
**Slug:** `ai-can-build-anything`
**Category:** Engineering Philosophy
**Tags:** Engineering Judgment, AI & Work, Traditional Industry, Digital Transformation
**Read time:** 5 min

**Thesis:** AI makes any technical solution fast to build, which makes "wrong technology choice" the most common and expensive mistake — because judging whether a solution actually solves the business problem comes from shipped experience, not theory, and AI only accelerated theory.

**Running example:** An auto parts distributor (three warehouses, 40k SKUs) rebuilds its inventory system with microservices, real-time sync, and a mobile app — when the real problem was the warehouse posting stock movements at 6 p.m.

**Key claims:**
- Implementation cost was an invisible governance brake; with it gone, technology selection needs deliberate discipline
- Pattern knowledge (how RFID/multi-warehouse sync works) is commoditized; situational knowledge (whether it works *here*) is scarcer
- AI builds the requirement as stated and never questions it — and requirements are usually a symptom dressed as a solution
- Invest in convergence (narrowing to the right solution), not breadth

**Editorial notes:** First article to use a Taiwan traditional-industry scenario throughout. No place names. First article produced ZH-first.

## August 2026 — When Code Is Free, What Does Software Engineering Actually Cost?

**Slug:** `when-code-is-free`
**Category:** Engineering Philosophy
**Tags:** Engineering Culture, AI & Work, Professional Identity, ROI
**Read time:** 8 min

**Thesis:** AI has collapsed the marginal cost of writing code. This doesn't make engineering cheaper — it inverts the bottleneck. The cost center moves from execution to judgment: problem definition, output evaluation, feedback loop quality, and architectural integrity. The profession hasn't disappeared; it has shed the automatable parts and kept the parts that require consequence-informed human judgment.

**Key claims:**
- Execution skill is now a commodity; it is no longer sufficient as a professional differentiator
- Building the wrong thing fast is not an improvement — AI makes the wrong thing buildable at unprecedented speed
- Four new cost centers: problem definition, judgment quality, feedback loop speed, architectural integrity
- Four professional capabilities that remain scarce: system intuition, problem framing, risk sensing, value reasoning
- Most organizations are still measuring and hiring for the automatable layer

**CTA:** AIDARIS helps engineering organizations navigate this shift — not just adopting AI tools, but reorienting how engineering judgment is developed, measured, and applied.

## July 2026 — Token Budgets Are Engineering Budgets. Treat Them That Way.

**Slug:** `token-budgets-are-engineering-budgets`
**Category:** AI & Engineering
**Tags:** AI Investment, Engineering Governance, Token Budget, ROI
**Read time:** 7 min

**Thesis:** Token spend is not an infrastructure cost — it is an engineering investment. Most teams can't explain what their tokens are buying, which makes it a governance failure, not a cost problem. The fix is mapping token spend to outcomes and treating it with the same discipline as any other engineering budget.

**Key claims:**
- "We spend $X/month on AI" is meaningless without knowing what value it produces
- Token budget decisions are already being made implicitly; the question is whether they're being made deliberately
- Framework: map spend to workflows → attach outcomes → set thresholds → review as a team
- The teams that will get ahead are those that treat token governance as an engineering discipline, not an accounting line
